Recent progress in atmospheric reanalysis products such as ERA5 has made it possible to study city-scale temperature trends without dedicated station networks. By sampling the nearest 0.25-degree grid cell at each city's coordinates, we obtain a homogeneous monthly time series spanning 1940 to the present. For a teaching pipeline, we restrict the analysis window to 1995–2024 (thirty complete years, matching the WMO climate normal definition) and apply ordinary least squares on annual means, with confidence intervals from a Student-t distribution rather than a normal approximation. Although this approach ignores well-known issues such as residual autocorrelation and the urban heat island effect, it remains a reasonable first pass that students can reproduce end-to-end in under thirty minutes using only the standard library plus pandas and matplotlib. The resulting trend estimates for seven Chinese cities cluster between +0.02 and +0.07 degrees Celsius per year, with confidence intervals that exclude zero in six cases.
